Discussion prototype · not a system design

One product. One source. Four consumers.

How regulatory facts for a construction chemical should move into an SDS, a declaration, a label, and a construction digital product passport — without a second copy. Illustrative product. Public regulation and public Sika programmes.

The passport is not a new document.

Regulation (EU) 2024/3110 already tells a manufacturer what the construction digital product passport must carry. Article 76 lists the declaration of performance and conformity, instructions and safety information, technical documentation, the label, unique identifiers, and other Union-law documents. Sika largely has that content. It does not yet have it as one product-type identity that a machine, a country, and a customer can trust.

Two histories

One product, more than one master

MBCC (Master Builders Construction Chemicals) was BASF’s construction-chemicals business. Sika acquired it in 2023, so many products still have two histories. Fast Forward, presented in November 2025, uses that integration to finish a group ERP rollout, with a public target around the beginning of 2027. A regulatory attribute that still lives in both will be published twice.

Documents first

SDS and DoP are outputs today

A public 2020 case study describes Sika’s document platform taking content from SAP Hybris PIM and Schema ST4, so staff see the approved file. That solves “which PDF.” The passport also needs the fields inside the PDF, kept current, and tied to the product type.

AI is already funded

Formulation models need the same ID

Sika has said publicly that formulation work on cement additives, mortars and admixtures is already using a large lab data set. An agent that drafts a declaration or blocks a substance is a consumer of regulatory data. It is not the place where that data is born.

Timing, said carefully: the regulation entered into force on 7 January 2025 and applies in phases, as new harmonised technical specifications are published. The construction passport system itself depends on a Commission act. The current public timetable points to Q2 2027, and the manufacturer duty follows 18 months after that act enters into force. There is no single date to put on a steerco slide. There is a data model to agree before that act arrives.

Four rules, agreed once.

Rule 1

Connect, do not copy

The label, the country SDS file, the DoPC and the passport read the attribute. They do not become a new home for it. A gap is written down. It is not filled by retyping.

Rule 2

Three owners, or a fight

A business owner defines the meaning. A system of record creates the value. A steward fixes quality. The passport, the artwork tool and any model are consumers. Mixing those roles is why ownership looks unclear.

Rule 3

Local is a key, not a second model

Language, legal entity, plant and a declared value per specification stay country- or plant-specific. They hang off the global ID. They do not justify another product master.

Rule 4

AI reads a trusted attribute or it does not run

An extraction agent is useful when the source is still a document and a person checks the result. It is the wrong design once the attribute has a source, an owner and a quality rule. Formulation models and passport assembly should consume the governed value. They should not invent a parallel one. Human review stays on the exception queue, not on every field.

Ninety days, on work already in flight.

The pilot attaches to the EU/EEA SDS load into SAP-EDMS. It does not start a new platform.

Why this family: admixtures already show up in Sika’s public formulation-AI examples, and they sit in construction-product regulation. If performance data has no source, that is the finding. The pilot is allowed to discover a gap.
