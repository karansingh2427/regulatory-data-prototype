For discussion · the method, not a new system

Write the fact once. Generate the pack from it.

The method used for digital crop-protection labels, applied to a construction product. No new system, no new database.

Start from the fact, not from the Word file.

Crop-protection products are sold in many countries. The pack label says what is in the product, how to use it, the hazard, and who may sell it. That text lived in Word files, PDFs, and shared folders. People copied it from one system to another. The change was to name each fact, give it one home, and generate the label from those facts.

  1. What must be the same in every country? The product, the substance, and the meaning of a hazard sentence.
  2. What must stay in the country? The language, the legal entity, the registration number, and the dose.
  3. Which system creates the fact? Product data in the material system. Safety in the safety data sheet. Approval in the registration record. The label only reads them.
  4. What is the smallest pilot? One country set, an agreed list of facts, and a label produced from that list. Local holes go on a gap list. They do not become a second copy.

A farmer can scan a code on the pack and see the current approved label information. That channel is AgriGuide, across 27 EU countries. The code was the easy part. The work was one structure, and keeping the digital text aligned with the approved label. If they differ, the approved label wins.

The construction pack asks the same four questions.

A construction chemical also needs a pack text, a safety data sheet, and a signed performance statement. Europe is adding a page a contractor can open from a code on the pack. That page is the digital product passport. It should read the same facts. It should not become another place where people retype them.

After the MBCC purchase, some products still have two histories. MBCC was formerly BASF’s construction-chemicals business; Sika closed the purchase in May 2023. Two histories means the same fact can be published twice. The rule stays the same: connect to the home, do not make a second one.

Two facts. Only one of them has a home I can name.

Ninety days, on work already moving.

The safety data sheets for the EU are already being stored in the document system. Start there. Do not start a new platform, and do not start with the country that only has Word files.

Scope One product family already in that safety-document work. About fifteen facts the passport will have to repeat. All countries. Artwork. A new database. A public passport.
For each fact Name the home, the person who defines the meaning, and the person who fixes bad data. If there is no home, write it on the gap list. Retyping the missing fact into a spreadsheet so the demo looks complete.
Show The safety facts can be read from the approved safety document. The performance number is on the gap list. A model filling in a performance number.

Two questions. When a safety document is stored, do you keep only the PDF, or also the individual facts? Which system creates the performance number that the declaration and the passport must show?

AgriGuide is the CropLife Europe digital-label channel: a farmer scans a code and sees the current approved use and safety information. The digital text does not replace the approved label. The construction passport comes from the digital product passport chapter of Regulation (EU) 2024/3110, which covers the declaration of performance, safety information, labelling, and product identifiers.
